# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English

Controlled English for technical documentation. Goal: one meaning per sentence,
readable by non-native speakers, cheap to translate. Apply to procedural and
descriptive technical text — not to narrative, marketing, or academic prose.

The official Dictionary (approved words + not-approved alternatives) is the
authority: <https://asd-ste100.org>. Examples below are illustrative.

## Words

- Use one word for one meaning, and one meaning for one word. Never use the
  same word as both noun and verb (`test the unit` / `do the test` — not
  `the test tests`).
- Prefer the short common word. `about` not `approximately`, `use` not
  `utilize`, `start` not `commence`/`initiate`, `get` not `obtain`, `before`
  not `prior to`, `make sure` not `ensure`, `obey` not `comply with`, `help`
  not `assist`, `let` not `permit`.
- Keep one term for one thing across the whole document. No synonyms for
  variety.
- Technical names and technical verbs specific to the domain are allowed even
  when not in the Dictionary. Define them once.
- No slang, jargon, idioms, metaphors, or humor.
- Noun clusters: three words maximum. Break longer ones with prepositions
  (`control valve of the fuel pump`, not `fuel pump control valve assembly`).

## Verbs

- Allowed forms: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple
  future, and past participle used as an adjective.
- No `-ing` forms (gerund or present participle) unless part of a technical
  name.
- Active voice in procedures, always. Passive voice only in descriptive text,
  and only when the agent is unknown or irrelevant.
- One instruction per sentence. Split compound steps.

## Sentences and paragraphs

- Procedural sentence: 20 words maximum. Descriptive sentence: 25 words
  maximum.
- Keep articles (`the`, `a`) — no telegraphic style.
- Paragraph: one topic, six sentences maximum in descriptive text.
- Start a paragraph with its topic sentence.
- Use a vertical list when a step has several conditions or sub-actions.

## Warnings and cautions

- Put the warning or caution *before* the step it applies to.
- Start with a clear command, then the reason:
  `Do not touch the connector. High voltage can kill you.`
- State the condition first when the action depends on it:
  `If the light is on, disconnect the power.`

## Checklist before finishing

1. Every sentence within the word limit.
2. No `-ing` verbs, no passive voice in procedures.
3. No word used in two parts of speech.
4. Same term for the same thing everywhere.
5. Noun clusters of three words or fewer.
6. Warnings placed before their steps.
